1. Executive Summary
FraudGraph Shield is a dual-engine, real-time fraud intelligence platform purpose-built to address the two most critical gaps in public sector bank fraud defenses: the inability to detect coordinated mule account networks, and the lack of a unified scoring system that fuses cross-channel behavioral signals with regulatory alerts.
The system combines a Graph Neural Network (GNN) that models money relay topologies across account networks with a high-velocity transaction scorer built on LightGBM. Both engines ingest live Government CFMS/I4C cyber fraud alerts, TMS flags, and cross-channel behavioral data. The output is a composite risk score with full SHAP-based explainability, enabling both automated blocking and analyst-assisted investigation — satisfying RBI regulatory requirements end-to-end.
Key Claims
Detection of mule account relay graphs before funds exit the bank ecosystem. Real-time transaction risk scoring in under 150 milliseconds per transaction. Unified ingestion of government CFMS alerts, TMS alerts, and cross-bank data streams. SHAP explainability for every decision — audit-ready and regulator-compliant.
2. Problem Statement and Context
2.1 The Mule Account Crisis in Indian Banking
Mule accounts are legitimate bank accounts — often owned by economically vulnerable individuals — that are recruited or coerced by criminal networks to receive and relay stolen funds. When a UPI/NEFT fraud occurs, the stolen money is rarely sent directly to the fraudster. Instead, it is rapidly hop-forwarded through 3 to 8 mule accounts before being cashed out or converted to cryptocurrency, a technique known as layering.
According to the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C), over 1.1 lakh mule accounts were frozen across public sector banks between January and October 2024. The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has documented that fraudulent UPI transactions alone crossed Rs. 1,457 crore in FY2024. The Government of India's Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFMS) registers over 2,000 new fraud complaints daily, a significant fraction of which involve mule account layering.
Why Existing Systems Fail
Rule-based fraud systems (velocity rules, amount thresholds) look at individual accounts in isolation. They cannot see that Account A received Rs. 50,000 from a fraud victim, forwarded Rs. 48,000 to Account B, which forwarded Rs. 45,000 to Account C within 4 hours. This relay graph structure is the signature of mule activity — and it is completely invisible to row-level ML models and rule engines operating on single-account data.

3.2 Key Feature Groups Identified
Through systematic analysis of the 3,924-column structure, the following semantically meaningful feature groups were identified:
Feature Group A: Behavioral Velocity Ratios (F1–F3600, approx.)
The dominant structure consists of repeating 6-column blocks representing behavioral ratios across multiple time windows (1-week, 1-month, 3-month lookbacks based on pattern analysis). These ratios capture how an account's transaction behavior compares to its own historical baseline and to segment peers. Ratio values near 1.0 indicate normal behavior; values significantly above or below 1.0 indicate anomalous velocity.

Feature Group B: Account Risk Indicators (F3880–F3899)
The columns near the end of the feature space encode account-level risk signals derived from historical scoring runs. Analysis revealed:
F3895–F3896: Credit score bucket (values: 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900). 73% of accounts fall in the 600 bucket, representing standard risk; the 400 and 500 buckets show elevated F3897 rates.
F3897: This column shows a distribution of 76.9% zero, 19.3% one, and 3.8% two-or-above — consistent with a risk tier label (clean / elevated / flagged). This will be used as the primary target variable for supervised training.
F3880–F3885: Normalized deviation scores (range approximately -1 to +2) representing how far account behavior deviates from segment peers. Strongly negative values (-0.99, -1.0) indicate accounts with no comparable peer behavior — a known anomaly indicator.

3.3 Class Imbalance Analysis and Strategy
The dataset exhibits moderate class imbalance with 23.1% of accounts carrying a risk label above zero. Across account types, MSME Medium accounts show the highest suspicious rate (37%) despite small absolute count, while MSME Micro accounts show the lowest (8%) — suggesting distinct risk profiles requiring segment-stratified models.
The preprocessing strategy uses a combination of Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) applied only to training folds during cross-validation, cost-sensitive learning with class weights inversely proportional to class frequency, and stratified k-fold cross-validation with k=5 to preserve class distributions across folds.

Occupation risk profile (binary mapping of occupation to empirical risk rate from training data)
Missingness pattern vector (binary mask of NA columns per account — the pattern itself encodes account type and vintage)
Peer deviation composite (mean of F3880–F3885 absolute deviation scores)
Product complexity score (count of active product flags from F3900–F3924 — high complexity in short-tenure accounts is anomalous)
4. Solution Architecture
4.1 High-Level System Design
FraudGraph Shield is a four-layer microservices architecture designed for modularity, allowing Bank of India to adopt individual components into its existing Core Banking System (CBS) infrastructure without requiring a complete replacement.
Architecture Principle
Each layer can be deployed and tested independently. Layer 1 (ingestion) can run without Layer 2 (detection). Layer 2 engines are independently deployable REST microservices. Layer 3 (fusion) consumes both via message queue. This reduces integration risk for a production bank deployment and allows phased rollout.
Layer 1: Multi-Source Data Ingestion
All data streams are normalized into a unified Account Event Record (AER) — a standardized JSON schema — before downstream processing. This abstraction layer decouples detection logic from data source specifics.
Data Source
Ingestion Method and Latency Target
Core Banking System (CBS) transaction feed
Apache Kafka topic subscription; event published per transaction commit. Target latency: under 50ms from CBS commit to Kafka topic.
Government CFMS / I4C fraud alert API
Polling adapter at 60-second intervals. CFMS alert linked to account via mobile number and account number hash. Alert enriches AER with fraud_ticket_flag and cfms_ticket_age_hours fields.

Direct Kafka bridge from TMS alert topic. TMS alert type and severity code mapped to standardized risk_indicator enum.

Batch job runs at T+15 minutes, aggregating UPI, NEFT, RTGS, mobile banking, and ATM events into account-level velocity counters published to a Redis cache.

Layer 2A: Graph Neural Network — Mule Account Network Detector
This engine models the entire bank's account network as a directed graph and uses a Graph Neural Network to identify accounts that occupy structurally suspicious positions — specifically, accounts that function as relay nodes in money-laundering subgraphs.
Graph Construction: Nodes represent accounts (one node per account, enriched with account feature vector from the dataset). Directed edges represent transaction flows, weighted by cumulative transferred amount and edge age. The graph is updated incrementally with each new transaction; historical edges beyond a 90-day window are pruned to maintain computational tractability.
GNN Architecture: The system uses a GraphSAGE (Graph Sample and Aggregation) variant with three message-passing layers. GraphSAGE was selected over Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) because it supports inductive learning — meaning it can classify newly opened accounts without retraining the entire model. Each layer aggregates feature information from sampled 1-hop, 2-hop, and 3-hop neighborhoods, allowing the model to detect relay patterns spanning up to three transaction hops.
Node features (per account): 64-dimensional embedding derived from the 3,924-feature dataset via PCA, plus account type, tenure, credit score, occupation risk score, and active CFMS alert flag
Edge features: Transaction amount (normalized), transaction channel, elapsed time since transaction, and is_same_bank_flag
Training objective: Semi-supervised node classification using labeled mule accounts (from historical freeze decisions) as positive examples and a random sample of long-tenure, low-velocity accounts as negative examples
Output: Per-account mule probability score (0.0 to 1.0) and subgraph cluster ID for co-suspicious accounts
4.1 (Unique Innovation) — Temporal Hop Velocity Signature (THVS)
Novel Approach: Temporal Hop Velocity Signature
Most graph-based fraud systems only look at structural topology (who is connected to whom). FraudGraph Shield adds a temporal dimension: the speed at which funds traverse hops. In legitimate transactions, money entering an account stays for hours to days before being forwarded. In mule accounts, the median time-to-forward is under 4 hours, and the amount forwarded is typically 90-98% of the received amount (net of small fees). The THVS feature captures both the hop speed distribution and the amount retention ratio across each account's transaction neighborhood. This signal is unique to this architecture and is not captured by any existing commercial fraud system.
Layer 2B: Real-Time Transaction Scorer
The transaction scorer evaluates each individual transaction within milliseconds of submission, producing a risk score that reflects how anomalous the transaction is relative to the account's historical behavior and segment peers.
Model: LightGBM gradient boosted decision trees with DART (Dropout Additive Regression Trees) sampling, selected for its sub-100ms inference time, native handling of missing values (critical given the 68% NA density in the dataset), and built-in feature importance for SHAP compatibility.

A second model — an Isolation Forest for unsupervised anomaly detection — runs in parallel as a complementary signal. Transactions flagged by both the LightGBM scorer and the Isolation Forest are immediately escalated to the highest-priority analyst queue regardless of composite score, implementing a fail-safe detection layer for novel fraud patterns not present in training data.
4.2 (Unique Innovation) — Adaptive Behavioral Baseline with Concept Drift Detection
Novel Approach: Per-Account Adaptive Baseline
Standard fraud models are trained once and applied statically until the next retraining cycle. This creates a known blind spot: accounts that gradually shift their behavior to mimic legitimate patterns over months before committing fraud (account aging/warming). FraudGraph Shield implements an online exponential moving average (EMA) baseline per account that updates with every transaction. A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on the score distribution runs daily to detect concept drift in the population. When drift is detected, the affected account segment is flagged for priority analyst review, and an accelerated retraining job is triggered. This makes the system self-updating without manual intervention.
Layer 3: Risk Fusion Engine
The fusion engine combines signals from both detection engines plus regulatory inputs into a single, interpretable composite risk score.
Composite Score Formula: The composite score is a weighted combination of the GNN mule probability score, the LightGBM transaction risk score, and the regulatory alert severity score. Weights are tuned during validation to optimize for precision at high recall thresholds, prioritizing reduction of false negatives (missed frauds) over false positives (wrongly blocked transactions) for accounts exceeding a moderate risk threshold.

Layer 3: SHAP Explainability Module
For every decision above medium risk, a SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis identifies and ranks the top 5 features driving the score. This explanation is attached to the analyst alert, enabling rapid human decision-making. SHAP explanations also serve as audit evidence for RBI compliance reviews, addressing the model explainability requirements of the RBI Master Direction on Digital Lending and upcoming regulatory guidance on AI use in financial services.
4.3 (Unique Innovation) — Cross-Bank Mule Identification via I4C Data Federation
Novel Approach: Federated Mule Intelligence
Mule account networks routinely span multiple banks. A money relay chain might use one account at Bank of India, one at SBI, and one at HDFC before cashing out. No single bank can see this full picture with its own data alone. FraudGraph Shield proposes a federated graph extension using I4C (Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre) as the trust anchor. When a transaction targets an account at another bank, the system queries the I4C national mule account registry via a hashed account identifier. If the destination account appears in the I4C registry or has active CFMS tickets, the outgoing transaction is immediately escalated regardless of the originating account's own score. This cross-bank signal is a capability no existing bank-level fraud system provides.
Layer 4: Feedback and Self-Improvement Loop
The system includes a structured feedback pipeline that transforms analyst decisions into labeled training examples, enabling continuous model improvement without manual retraining overhead.
Analyst confirms fraud: Transaction and account record added to positive class training set; account features cached for next retraining cycle
Analyst clears false positive: Record added to hard-negative training set with elevated sample weight to penalize that feature pattern in future training
Case outcome confirmed (account prosecuted, PMLA case filed): Record added to high-confidence positive set with 2x sample weight
Monthly retraining: Full model retraining triggered on first Monday of each month using accumulated labeled data; models promoted to production only if AUC-ROC on holdout test set meets or exceeds current production model performance
Concept drift trigger: Unscheduled retraining triggered when KS-test p-value drops below 0.05 on any score distribution segment
5. Technical Methodology
5.1 Machine Learning Pipeline
Data Preprocessing
Load raw dataset (9,082 rows x 3,924 features). Validate schema and row count integrity.
Handle missing values: For velocity ratio features, replace NA with 0.5 (representing no deviation from baseline, a conservative neutral assumption). For binary flag features, replace NA with 0. Generate a missingness indicator feature vector per account.
Extract metadata columns (F3886–F3924) as separate interpretable features. Parse F3888 (account opening date) to compute account tenure in days.
Apply segment-stratified z-score normalization to velocity ratio features separately for each account type to prevent distribution leakage across segments.
Construct interaction features: credit score bucket x occupaton risk score, account tenure x velocity deviation, CFMS alert flag x transaction risk tier.
Perform SMOTE on training folds only (never on validation or test folds) to address the 23.1% minority class imbalance.
Model Training — GNN Mule Detector
Construct account transaction graph from dataset features using account metadata as node features and inferred transaction relationships from correlated velocity features as edge proxies for the training prototype.
Train GraphSAGE model with 3 message-passing layers, 128-dimensional hidden embeddings, ReLU activations, and dropout rate of 0.3 for regularization.
Use semi-supervised node classification loss: Binary cross-entropy on labeled nodes, augmented with an unsupervised graph reconstruction loss on unlabeled nodes.
Evaluate using node-level AUC-ROC, precision, recall, and F1 score on a 20% held-out test graph partition.
Model Training — Transaction Scorer
Perform 5-fold stratified cross-validation with LightGBM. Hyperparameter tuning via Bayesian optimization on validation fold AUC-ROC.
Key hyperparameters: num_leaves in range 31–127, max_depth in range 4–8, learning_rate in range 0.01–0.1, feature_fraction in range 0.6–0.9, class_weight as balanced.
Extract SHAP feature importance after final training; select top 200 features by mean absolute SHAP value for production inference to reduce latency.
Train complementary Isolation Forest on transaction features for unsupervised anomaly detection. contamination parameter set to 0.05 based on estimated fraud prevalence.

6. Regulatory Alignment and Compliance
6.1 Applicable Regulatory Framework
FraudGraph Shield is designed with explicit compliance against the following regulatory frameworks governing fraud detection and reporting in Indian public sector banks:
RBI Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC), 2016 (updated 2023): The system's risk tier classification (Low/Medium/High/Critical) aligns with the RBI's risk-based KYC categorization framework. High and Critical risk accounts are automatically escalated for enhanced due diligence (EDD) review.
Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, Section 12: All transaction blocking actions and STR filings are logged with full audit trails meeting PMLA record-keeping requirements. Reports are generated in the format prescribed by the Financial Intelligence Unit India (FIU-IND).
RBI Circular on Fraud Risk Management (RBI/2024-25/45): The system's escalation thresholds, analyst review timelines, and reporting workflows are designed to comply with the Early Warning Signals (EWS) framework specified in this circular.
I4C / CFMS Integration Guidelines: Account freeze actions triggered by Critical-risk scores include automatic generation of a CFMS complaint ticket in the format specified by the Ministry of Home Affairs I4C technical integration guide.
Information Technology Act, 2000, Section 43A (Data Protection): All account data is processed within the bank's own infrastructure. No raw account data leaves the perimeter. I4C queries use one-way hashed identifiers. SHAP explanations contain only feature importance values, not raw transaction amounts.
6.2 Explainability for Regulatory Audit
A significant advantage of the FraudGraph Shield architecture over deep learning black-box approaches is full auditability. Every automated action — transaction blocking, account freezing, STR filing — is accompanied by a SHAP explanation document that can be produced on demand for RBI supervisory reviews. 8. Innovation Summary and Competitive Differentiation
8.1 Three Novel Contributions
FraudGraph Shield introduces three innovations that distinguish it from existing commercial fraud detection solutions and from expected competing submissions:
Innovation 1: Temporal Hop Velocity Signature (THVS)
Existing graph-based fraud systems detect structural anomalies (unusual connectivity patterns). THVS adds a temporal dimension by quantifying how rapidly money traverses each hop in a relay chain and what fraction of the received amount is forwarded. This combination of topology and timing creates a signature that is extremely difficult for fraudsters to mimic without abandoning the economic utility of the mule network.
Innovation 2: Adaptive Per-Account Behavioral Baseline
Rather than comparing accounts to static population averages, the system maintains a continuously updated exponential moving average baseline for each account. This prevents the account-warming attack pattern where fraudsters operate accounts legitimately for months to avoid triggering static rules before using them for fraud. The concept drift detection layer ensures the system identifies shifts in fraud patterns proactively.
Innovation 3: Cross-Bank Federated Mule Intelligence via I4C
By querying the I4C national mule account registry for destination accounts in outgoing transactions, the system extends its detection radius beyond the bank's own account base. This is the first student-level proposal to explicitly architect this cross-institution detection capability using the government's existing I4C infrastructure — a capability that aligns precisely with the hackathon's focus on collaboration between banks and government institutions.
